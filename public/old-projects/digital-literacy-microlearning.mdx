## Overview

This project addressed a delivery problem that shows up in public-facing programs:

> How do you support learners who need digital skills **without requiring them to enroll** in a formal course or navigate a complex platform?

The solution was a set of small, focused learning resources that could be shared directly—by staff, at the moment of need.

---

## The challenge

Learners with low digital literacy often face barriers that make traditional online courses hard to access:

- unclear starting points (“I don’t even know what I don’t know”)
- low confidence and fear of “breaking something”
- limited time and limited device familiarity
- fragmented learning needs (email today, portals tomorrow)

At the same time, staff needed something reliable they could point to quickly.

---

## The solution

A microlearning series was designed around:

- short, concrete lessons with a single outcome
- plain-language explanations
- practice-oriented steps learners could follow immediately
- modular topics that staff could share selectively

The series was designed as a **public resource**, so it could be used flexibly wherever learners showed up.

---

## Highlights

### Designed for frictionless sharing
Staff could direct learners to specific lessons as just-in-time support.

### Low-cognitive-load structure
Each lesson focused on one task, with clear success criteria.

### Built for scale
A lightweight format supported broad reuse without specialized facilitation.
