## Overview

This project focused on a practical delivery constraint that shows up in national-scale training:

> How do you deliver a large, complex curriculum **fast**, without sacrificing accuracy—and then keep improving it once it’s live?

Rather than treating “launch” as the finish line, the design treated launch as the beginning of an **iteration cycle** grounded in learner feedback.

---

## The challenge

Genetics education is conceptually dense and often unfamiliar to learners. At the same time, the training needed to be:

- accurate and policy-aligned
- deliverable on a tight timeline
- modular enough to update as requirements evolved
- usable by a broad audience with varying background knowledge

---

## The solution

The curriculum was built using an agile-inspired workflow:

- rapid prototyping of learning objects
- structured review with subject-matter experts
- release in usable increments
- continuous refinement from learner signals (questions, performance patterns, feedback)

Content was designed as **updateable modules** rather than monolithic courses, enabling improvements without reworking the entire experience.

---

## Highlights

### Speed without shortcuts
A fast delivery cycle focused effort on what learners needed most, first.

### Iteration as a feature
Feedback loops were treated as part of the learning system, not a post-launch afterthought.

### Modular, updateable structure
The design supported continuous improvement without disrupting learners.
