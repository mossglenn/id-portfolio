## Overview

This project explores a design question that shows up in a lot of professional learning:

> How do you keep learners engaged while practicing a complex, multi-step skill—especially when “practice problems” usually deliver feedback only after the whole attempt is complete?

---

## The challenge

Statistics problems are often *procedural* and *multi-step*. Traditional problem sets tend to overload learners because they must complete several steps before they get any meaningful feedback.

When the final answer is wrong, the learner often can’t tell **which step** caused the error—so they restart from the beginning, burn time, and lose motivation.

The core design challenge was to build something that:

- keeps learners motivated long enough to persist
- reduces “restart from scratch” frustration
- identifies *where* understanding breaks down
- helps learners recover quickly and keep moving

---

## The solution

Rather than simply digitizing problem sets, the tutor uses a **game scenario** to make practice feel purposeful, while providing **real-time, diagnostic feedback** during the attempt—not just at the end.

### Two scenario formats

**Scenario 1: Escape Room**
Learners complete steps toward a correct solution to progress through the scenario, with feedback that supports correction *before* the whole attempt fails.

**Scenario 2: Treasure Hunt Adventure**
A second narrative structure that supports the same learning goals, but varies the pacing and reward loop to maintain engagement across repeated practice.

### Personalized, adaptive feedback

The tutor looks for indicators that the learner is “going off track,” then:

- confirms the likely misconception
- provides corrective feedback aligned to that diagnosis
- refocuses attention on the specific part of the process that needs repair

This helps learners spend **time and energy on the parts they’re still learning**, instead of repeatedly redoing what they already understand.

---

## Highlights

### Enhanced motivation
Gamification boosts motivation by integrating an engaging storyline and meaningful progress—so the learner has a reason to persist through difficult steps.

### Diagnostic interactivity
The experience is designed to detect “wrong-turn” patterns in a multi-step process and deliver corrective feedback while the learner still has context.

### Reduced cognitive load
A structured scenario breaks complexity into smaller chunks and reduces overwhelm, helping learners stay oriented as problems become more complex.
