## Overview

This project focused on helping instructional designers move beyond surface-level personalization and toward **evidence-based adaptive learning strategies**.

Rather than presenting adaptive learning as a technology feature, the instruction emphasized **design decisions** that influence how learners experience feedback, pacing, and content sequencing.

---

## The challenge

Many adaptive learning tools promise personalization, but designers often lack clear guidance on:

- what should adapt
- when adaptation is beneficial
- how to interpret learner signals

Without this clarity, adaptive systems risk becoming expensive versions of traditional branching.

---

## The solution

The instructional experience translated research findings into **concrete design patterns**, including:

- adaptive feedback timing
- confidence-based remediation
- mastery-triggered progression

Examples demonstrated how small design shifts can significantly improve learning efficiency and engagement.

---

## Highlights

### Research-to-practice translation
Complex research findings were converted into usable design heuristics.

### Design-first framing
The focus remained on instructional decisions, not platform features.

### Transfer-oriented examples
Scenarios illustrated how principles apply across tools and content types.
