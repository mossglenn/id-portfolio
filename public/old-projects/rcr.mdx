## Overview

This project tackled a common failure mode in compliance training:

> How can compliance training support ethical decision-making—rather than just checking completion boxes?

The goal was to build training that helped researchers understand their role in protecting participants and maintaining the integrity of research.

---

## The challenge

Traditional compliance training often leads learners to:

- memorize rules without context
- miss how policies apply to real workflow situations
- disengage because the content feels detached from actual practice

For research programs, that disengagement can translate into risk.

---

## The solution

The curriculum was designed to emphasize:

- concrete scenarios grounded in realistic research decisions
- clear connections between policy and day-to-day actions
- assessment designs that detect misconceptions (not just recall)
- a structured path to certification for access to restricted data tiers

---

## Highlights

### Meaningful scenarios
Learners practice decisions they are likely to face, not abstract definitions.

### Policy-aligned accuracy
Content reflects current guidance while remaining accessible.

### Credentialed pathway
Courses lead to certification, reinforcing accountability and readiness.
