## Overview

This project sits at the intersection of learning science and game development:

> How can a game make abstract concepts learnable—without turning the experience into disguised worksheets?

The work required translating research-driven learning principles into mechanics that felt like real play.

---

## The challenge

Designing for learning in games creates a persistent tension:

- games reward speed, experimentation, and exploration
- learning goals often require reflection, explanation, and correction
- “fun” mechanics can easily conflict with conceptual understanding

The challenge was to design experiences that preserved authentic gameplay while still supporting reliable learning outcomes.

---

## The solution

The learning games were designed as **inquiry-driven experiences** in which players:

- encounter a problem or mystery
- test ideas through interaction
- interpret feedback from the system
- refine strategies based on evidence

Learning objectives were embedded into mechanics, not appended as quizzes.

---

## Highlights

### Learning goals expressed as mechanics
Core concepts were reinforced through what players *had to do* to succeed.

### Iterative testing and refinement
Gameplay and learning outcomes were refined through repeated playtesting cycles.

### Cross-disciplinary translation
Success depended on aligning learning scientists, designers, artists, and engineers on a shared intent.

---

## My role

I served as a **learning design lead and translator**, responsible for:

- aligning instructional goals with gameplay systems
- facilitating collaboration across learning science and game development teams
- guiding user testing to validate both engagement and learning
